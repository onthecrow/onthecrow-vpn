Privacy Policy

OnthecrowVPN · Last updated: 2 August 2026

OnthecrowVPN has no accounts and shows no advertising. Your configurations never leave your device, and your traffic goes to the VPN server you choose — with one default exception: Google Play Services is sent directly, outside the tunnel, so notifications keep arriving. You can change that in Settings. The app also includes Firebase for crash reporting and usage analytics, described below. This page explains what the app does with information.

What the app is

OnthecrowVPN is a VPN client. It routes your device's traffic through a server that you configure, using a configuration you supply or subscribe to. We do not operate those servers, and we have no visibility into the traffic that passes through them.

Information the app stores on your device

  • Your configurations — the server details you add, and which one is selected.
  • Your settings — split tunnelling choices (which apps bypass or use the VPN) and notification preferences.
  • A diagnostic log — see the section below.

All of this stays in the app's own storage on your device. Uninstalling the app removes it.

Information the app sends

  • To your VPN server: your traffic, encrypted. That is the point of a VPN. What the operator of that server can see is governed by their policy, not ours.
  • To a subscription URL you enter: if you add a configuration by URL, the app fetches it from that address. If the address you provide uses plain http rather than https, that fetch — including the identifier in the URL — is sent unencrypted to the host you specified. Prefer https subscription links.
  • To our configuration service: when you add a subscription, the app asks a Google Firebase (Firestore) database whether a configuration bundle with the identifier you entered exists, and retrieves it. This request carries the identifier you typed and the usual network metadata any HTTPS request carries, such as your IP address. It does not carry your identity, your traffic, or any browsing information.
  • To Firebase (Google): the app includes the Firebase SDK for crash reporting (see below) and usage analytics: which screens and settings you use, whether a configuration was added and by which method (never its contents), whether a connection attempt succeeded or failed, roughly how long a session lasted, and coarse counts such as how many configurations you have. Every value is a fixed category or a range, never an exact number or a free-form string. It does not include your configurations, your traffic, your browsing, the address of any server, any credential, or the contents of your diagnostic log. Firebase Analytics may use an advertising identifier; the app shows no advertising and does not use it to advertise to you. See Firebase's privacy documentation for how Google handles this data.

Diagnostic log

The app keeps a rolling technical log on your device to make connection problems diagnosable. It records events such as when the tunnel connects, drops and reconnects, network changes, and errors reported by the VPN engine. On debug builds it also records the destination addresses of proxied connections; release builds do not.

This log never leaves your device unless you send it. There is no automatic upload. Settings contains a version row; long-pressing it opens the system share sheet so you can send the log to us — or anywhere else — if you are reporting a problem. Please look at it before you share it. The file is capped in size and older entries are discarded.

Crash reporting

Builds distributed through app stores include Firebase Crashlytics. It sends a report when the app crashes, and also when the app catches an unexpected internal error and keeps running. A report contains the error type, a stack trace, a broad category label such as “vpn_tunnel”, the device model, and the OS version. The error’s own message is deliberately removed before sending, because such a message can quote a server address or a credential. It does not send your configurations, your traffic, or your diagnostic log. See Firebase's privacy documentation for how Google handles that data.

Permissions and why

  • VPN service — required to create the tunnel. Android asks for your explicit consent the first time.
  • Notifications — Android requires a VPN to show an ongoing notification while it is running.
  • Query installed apps — to show you a list of apps in the split-tunnelling screen. The list is read on your device and never sent anywhere.

Children

The app is not directed at children. It has no accounts and collects no personal information from any user; the diagnostic and analytics data described above is collected from everyone who uses the app, children included.
