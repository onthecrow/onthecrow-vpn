Privacy Policy

OnthecrowVPN · Last updated: 20 July 2026

OnthecrowVPN does not collect, store, or transmit your personal data. There are no accounts, no advertising, and no analytics that track what you do. This page explains what the app does with information, which is mostly: nothing leaves your device.

What the app is

OnthecrowVPN is a VPN client. It routes your device's traffic through a server that you configure, using a configuration you supply or subscribe to. We do not operate those servers, and we have no visibility into the traffic that passes through them.

Information the app stores on your device

  • Your configurations — the server details you add, and which one is selected.
  • Your settings — split tunnelling choices (which apps bypass or use the VPN) and notification preferences.
  • A diagnostic log — see the section below.

All of this stays in the app's own storage on your device. Uninstalling the app removes it.

Information the app sends

  • To your VPN server: your traffic, encrypted. That is the point of a VPN. What the operator of that server can see is governed by their policy, not ours.
  • To our configuration service: when you add a subscription, the app asks a Google Firebase (Firestore) database whether a configuration bundle with the identifier you entered exists, and retrieves it. This request carries the identifier you typed and the usual network metadata any HTTPS request carries, such as your IP address. It does not carry your identity, your traffic, or any browsing information.
  • Nothing else. The app has no analytics SDK and shows no advertising.

Diagnostic log

The app keeps a rolling technical log on your device to make connection problems diagnosable. It records events such as when the tunnel connects, drops and reconnects, network changes, and errors reported by the VPN engine. On debug builds it also records the destination addresses of proxied connections; release builds do not.

This log never leaves your device unless you send it. There is no automatic upload. Settings contains a version row; long-pressing it opens the system share sheet so you can send the log to us — or anywhere else — if you are reporting a problem. Please look at it before you share it. The file is capped in size and older entries are discarded.

Crash reporting

Builds distributed through app stores may include Firebase Crashlytics. If present, it sends a crash report when the app crashes: the stack trace, the device model, and the OS version. It does not send your configurations, your traffic, or your diagnostic log. See Firebase's privacy documentation for how Google handles that data.

Permissions and why

  • VPN service — required to create the tunnel. Android asks for your explicit consent the first time.
  • Notifications — Android requires a VPN to show an ongoing notification while it is running.
  • Ignore battery optimisations — optional, and only so the tunnel can be restored reliably while the device is idle.
  • Query installed apps — to show you a list of apps in the split-tunnelling screen. The list is read on your device and never sent anywhere.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone, including children.
